Domain valuation is not guesswork. Professional buyers, brokers, and investors use the same handful of inputs every time,
and once you understand them you can sanity-check almost any asking price — including the **$75,000 asking price for
[VidaEnLaPlaya.com](/)**.

## Extension: .com is still the default

`.com` remains the extension buyers trust, type, and remember. Country codes (`.es`, `.mx`) and new extensions can work
for local or niche plays, but for a brand with international ambition the .com is the asset. Two otherwise identical
names can differ by an order of magnitude purely on extension.

## Length and word count

Shorter is better, but word *quality* beats raw character count. A natural two-word phrase such as `vacationrentals` is
far more valuable than a six-letter string of no meaning, because the phrase already has demand attached to it. Human
memory favours combinations that could plausibly be spoken as a sentence.

## Keyword and phrase demand

Valuers look at how often the phrase is searched, how commercial that search intent is, and how expensive the paid
results are. "Beach life" style phrases sit at the intersection of travel, real estate, and lifestyle — three of the
most commercially competitive categories in advertising.

## Brandability

Ask three questions: Is it easy to spell? Is it easy to say in more than one language? Does it evoke something
immediately? Names that pass all three are scarce, and scarcity is the single biggest driver of premium pricing.

## Comparable sales

Public records give the market its floor and ceiling. Well-documented transactions include:

- **VacationRentals.com** — $35,000,000 (2007)
- **Voice.com** — $30,000,000 (2019, announced in an SEC filing)
- **Hotels.com** — approximately $11,000,000 (2001)
- **Vacation.Rentals** — $500,300 (2018, a record for its extension)

Most premium sales happen privately, so public data understates the market — but it is the only data a buyer can verify
independently.

## Strategic value to a specific buyer

The same name can be worth 10x more to one buyer than another. A developer launching a coastal resort community gets
branding, direct type-in traffic, and an instantly credible email domain from a single purchase. That offset against a
lifetime of paid acquisition is what turns a "nice name" into a strategic acquisition.

## Putting it together

Score any domain on those six factors and the range usually narrows quickly. Names that score highly on extension,
natural phrasing, commercial demand, multilingual brandability, and clean comparables are exactly the ones that sit in
the mid-to-high five figures and above.
