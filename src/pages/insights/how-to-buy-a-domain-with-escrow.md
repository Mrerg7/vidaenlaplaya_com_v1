Buying a five-figure domain does not require a lawyer on retainer, but it does require a process. Escrow exists precisely
so that neither side has to trust the other — the money and the domain move only when both conditions are met.

## Step 1 — Make contact and agree on price

Start with a clear message: who you are, what the domain is for, and what you are willing to pay. Serious inquiries get
answers fast — for [VidaEnLaPlaya.com](/), every serious offer receives a personal response within 24 hours. Use the
[offer form](/#offer) or email `sales@desertrich.com` directly.

## Step 2 — Confirm the terms in writing

Before money moves, put four things in writing: the agreed price, the escrow provider, the transfer method, and the
timeline. A short confirmation email from both parties is usually enough for a standard transaction.

## Step 3 — Open an escrow transaction

A reputable third party — **Escrow.com**, an attorney escrow service, or your own counsel — holds the funds. The buyer
pays into escrow; the seller sees the funds are secured but cannot withdraw them until the transfer completes. Typical
fees are a small percentage of the price, usually capped.

Never wire money directly to a seller you have not verified, and never pay by gift card, crypto to an unknown wallet, or
any method that cannot be reversed.

## Step 4 — Transfer the domain

Once escrow confirms the funds, the seller provides the **auth code** (an EPP transfer code) or pushes the name to a
registrar account you specify. The domain moves to your account while remaining locked against sale to anyone else.

For most standard transfers this completes within **1–3 business days** after funds clear.

## Step 5 — Confirm and release

Verify three things before releasing escrow:

1. The domain appears in *your* registrar account.
2. The lock is removed and the name is under your control.
3. Administrative contact details point to your email.

Escrow then releases payment to the seller, and the transaction is closed.

## Extra safeguards worth taking

- Check the domain's history: registrar age, prior use, and any trademark conflicts.
- Use an HTTPS site and confirm you are corresponding with the address published by the seller.
- Ask for a simple purchase agreement if the price is significant.

## The short version

Offer → agree in writing → fund escrow → transfer by auth code → verify → release. Four days, no exposure, no trust
required.
